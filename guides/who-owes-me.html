When the debt crosses market day

Kejetia, Makola, Kaneshie, Techiman: most credit is promised "on market day" because that is when money comes. Three rules that work:

  1. Write the market day, not "later". "Techiman Thursday" is a date.
  2. Remind the evening before. By market day morning her money is already promised to five other people.
  3. Take "small small". GH₵ 20 today and GH₵ 30 next week beats GH₵ 50 never. Write each part; the book must show what is left, not what was.

What GRA now expects from a small trader

Since the GRA Modified Taxation Scheme, small businesses are asked to keep a daily sales book that covers cash, mobile money, bank and credit sales, and to declare actual sales for the year by 30 April on a simplified return, paying through *880#. A credit sale is a sale on the day it happens, not on the day you are paid. If your book only has cash, your year's figure is wrong. Which band am I in, and what is my annual sales figure?

Questions traders ask

What should I write when a customer buys on credit?

Four things: her name, what she took, the amount in cedis, and the day she promised. See the table above.

How do I remind a customer who owes me money without spoiling the relationship?

A short written message the day after the promised date, with the amount and the item. Not in front of other customers.

What if she pays part of it?

Write the part and the day. The book must always show what is left. In CountMy: "Ama paid me 100" and the app tells you what remains.

Should I stop giving credit?

No trader in Ghana stops; credit is how regular customers stay regular. Stop giving credit to the people whose page never gets shorter. You only know who they are if you have the page.

Does GRA need me to record credit sales?

Yes. The daily sales book under the Modified Taxation Scheme includes credit sales, and the annual figure you declare by 30 April is total sales, however you were paid.
